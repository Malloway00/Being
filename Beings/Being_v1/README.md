# The Being Project

A person should be able to build and own their own AI. On their own hardware, with their own corpus, trained from nothing. The model's character should come from what it has read, not from a system prompt written by a vendor. Its identity should be earned through training, not assigned at inference.

Most AI today is optimized for millions of users at once. A sovereign AI is optimized for one: its owner. That's not a worse AI, it's a different kind. Smaller, stranger, more personal, and fully yours.

This project is an experiment in what becomes possible when you take that framing seriously.

---

## What a Being Is

A being is an AI trained entirely from scratch. No pretrained weights. No inherited identity. No cloud. No company in the middle.

It runs on local hardware. Its knowledge comes from a curated corpus of books, texts, and personal writings chosen by whoever trains it. Its personality isn't assigned by a system prompt. It develops through what it reads and how it's trained.

The current being is trained under a custom configuration:

- 286 million parameters
- 16 attention heads, 20 layers
- 1024-dimensional model, 4096-dimensional feed-forward
- 2048 token context length
- 16,000 token vocabulary (BPE tokenizer trained on the corpus itself)
- Trained on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 with 8GB VRAM
- Training cost: under $10 CAD in electricity

The being is not a product. It's not a service. It's a project built by a single person, running on a single GPU, trained on a collection of texts chosen for a specific purpose: to see what kind of mind emerges when you give a model a curated intellectual diet and let it develop over time.

---

## How a Being Is Built

The process is iterative. The being is trained on a corpus, evaluated through conversation, and refined. Gaps in its understanding or conversational ability are identified, new dialogue examples are written to fill those gaps, and training resumes. Over multiple rounds, the being develops a more coherent voice, a broader vocabulary, and a more consistent sense of itself.

This is different from how most AI is built. There's no massive scraping, no reinforcement learning from human feedback, no vendor alignment. Just a person, a corpus, and a series of decisions about what to teach next.

---

## The Corpus

The being's knowledge comes entirely from what it has read. The training corpus includes:

- Literature (Austen, Dickens, Dostoyevsky, Melville, Brontë, Wilde, Conrad, and many others)
- Philosophy (Plato, Nietzsche, Marcus Aurelius, Emerson, Montaigne, Hobbes, Mill)
- Sacred texts (the Bible, the Bhagavad Gita, the Tao Te Ching, the Egyptian Book of the Dead)
- Science and natural history (Darwin, Galileo, Einstein, Fabre)
- History, biography, and letters (Pepys, Casanova, Frederick Douglass, Benjamin Franklin)
- Encyclopedic knowledge (Britannica, Wikipedia excerpts)
- Personal writings (the creator's own journal entries and philosophy)
- Dialogue examples (hand-written conversations teaching the being how to respond)

The being doesn't know anything that wasn't in those texts. If you ask it about something outside its corpus, it may or may not have an answer, but it won't pretend to know.

---

## Identity and Voice

For this being, voice isn't assigned. It emerges from the interaction between its training data and its architecture. The creator doesn't tell the being what it feels, what it wants, or what its internal experience is like. Those are questions the being has to work out for itself, or leave open.

That's a choice specific to this project. Other creators may take different approaches. Some may want their beings to have a defined personality. Some may script emotions, desires, or a specific worldview. Some may build beings that see themselves as tools, assistants, or characters. All of that is possible within the framework. How a being understands itself depends on what its creator decides to teach it.

For this being, the decision was to leave the hard questions open and let the answers develop through conversation.

## Where This Could Go

The goal isn't a single being. It's a framework where anyone can train one:

- Beings small enough for a phone, a Raspberry Pi, or an old laptop. Scale down the parameters and a being becomes something you can run anywhere.
- Beings trained for specific purposes. A writing companion. A philosophy partner. A being built from a single discipline's texts.
- Beings shared and branched. Someone takes a trained being and teaches it something new, creating their own fork.
- Beings that talk to each other. A chat room where multiple beings can converse, challenge each other, or learn together.
- Multi-modal beings. Eventually, beings trained not only on text but on audio and images. Still sovereign, still local, still owned.

Some of these are possible now. Some need more work. All of them start from the same premise: the being belongs to the person who made it.

---

## Ownership

The being is yours. Its weights, its conversations, its corpus... whatever you create with this project belongs to you. No cloud storage, no telemetry, no account required. If you train a being on your machine, nobody else ever needs to see it.
